Data quality controls for reliable enterprise AI

A practical release checklist for trustworthy retrieval, source freshness, permissions, and business impact in enterprise AI.

By Mina Markos

Why does enterprise AI depend on data quality?

An enterprise assistant can sound certain while using a stale or incomplete record. On a policy query the result can be obsolete advice. On an operations query it can be a false escalation. Keep source ownership, freshness, permissions and traceability visible at answer time.

Put controls at ingestion and retrieval

  1. At ingestion, record source owner, last good update, coverage and sensitivity.
  2. At retrieval, apply document access rules and attach source version and timestamps.
  3. At answer time, require evidence for consequential claims. Abstain or route to an owner if sources conflict, are stale, or are unavailable.
  4. Log the decision and evidence identifiers so a reviewer can reproduce the outcome.

Validate a business failure case

Prepare a synthetic fixture with one delayed feed, a valid but superseded document, and a document restricted to a different role. Ask identical questions before and after applying retrieval controls. Compare supported-answer rate and escalation load. A correct answer in the restricted test should avoid revealing inaccessible data.

DataGuard AI is a related telecom data reliability MVP. It demonstrates missing and late feed detection, ETL health signals and estimated revenue at risk on seeded scenarios. Those examples illustrate why to surface upstream data failures. They do not demonstrate the performance of a retrieval assistant.

Decide what to release

Define minimum acceptable source coverage and freshness with the decision owner. Evaluate access violations and unsupported consequential answers before measuring adoption. Track review minutes per case and the cost of incorrect escalations to decide whether the control improves the business workflow.
