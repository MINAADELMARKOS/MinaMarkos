Engineering Reliability as a Business Capability

Use service objectives, recovery drills, ownership, and error-budget decisions to make dependable operations an explicit investment.

By Mina Adel Markos · Published 17 November 2026 · 3 min read

Reliability is the ability to keep a promise

A dashboard that loads quickly but shows yesterday’s inventory is available in one sense and unusable in another. Define the promise at the user boundary: a fresh dataset, a completed payment, an accurate recommendation, or a recoverable publication. Infrastructure uptime is an input to that promise, not its full definition.

Ask the business owner which failures are tolerable, for how long, and with what fallback. An internal exploration tool and a time-critical operational feed need different service levels. Applying the strictest objective everywhere spends money and attention without a corresponding benefit.

Turn objectives into operating choices

Choose a service-level indicator that reflects the promise, such as the fraction of critical publications delivered correctly before their cutoff. Define the measurement window and exclusions before an incident. Include how correctness is checked and what happens when the check itself is unavailable.

An error budget can guide release and reliability work when its policy is agreed. If the budget is exhausted, the response might be reducing risky changes, fixing recurring defects, or improving recovery. It should not become a ritual percentage disconnected from user harm. Review whether alerts identify actionable threats early enough to matter.

Recovery is a practiced capability

A written runbook has value only if responders can use it with their actual permissions and tools. Exercise restoration, replay, rollback, and reconciliation in a safe environment. Record elapsed time, missing access, ambiguous steps, and remaining data gaps. A failed drill is useful evidence before the real outage.

Design ownership across team boundaries. A data product may depend on source applications, platform storage, transformation code, and downstream publication. Name the incident coordinator and escalation path. Without that agreement, each component can appear healthy while the end-to-end service stays broken.

Make the investment case with outcomes

Measure recurrence, detection time, time to restore usable service, manual work, and missed business deadlines. Compare those outcomes with the cost of on-call coverage, spare capacity, tooling, and engineering time. Avoid promising zero incidents; show which failure modes the investment reduces and how recovery becomes more predictable.

Review reliability work after implementation. If an alert never changes an action, revise or remove it. If spare capacity does not clear the expected backlog within the recovery objective, the model needs updating. Reliability becomes a business capability when the organization can choose a service promise and repeatedly demonstrate that it can keep or restore it.
